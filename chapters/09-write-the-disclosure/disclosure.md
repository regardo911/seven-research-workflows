# The disclosure statement

Use Elsevier's own sentence rather than a paraphrase. It is the most explicit of the
three and it satisfies the most readers.

```
Declaration of Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process

During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [TOOL] in order to [SPECIFIC
TASKS: e.g. generate configuration files and analysis scripts from author-specified
parameters; perform data cleaning under an integrity reconciliation; draft
explanatory text]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited
the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the
publication.
```

Fill the second bracket with what you actually did, specifically. "Assisted with the
manuscript" is not a disclosure, it is an evasion, and it is worse than saying nothing
because it looks like compliance.

## Where it goes

| Target venue | Where the disclosure goes | What it must contain |
|---|---|---|
| Elsevier journal | A section titled "Declaration of Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process", at the end of the manuscript immediately above the references | Tool, reason, and the statement that authors reviewed, edited and take full responsibility. Grammar and spelling checks exempt. |
| ICMJE-following journal | Cover letter AND the submitted work, in the appropriate section | Same content, in both places. No AI as author. No AI output cited as a primary source. |
| Nature Portfolio | No section named. Disclose against the risk tier of what you actually did, and state where you put it. | Which tier your use falls in, what the AI did, and who is accountable. Nothing red, nothing undisclosed. |
| Anywhere else | Check the specific journal | Default to the Elsevier shape: it is the most explicit and satisfies the most readers. |

**Nature's live policy names no manuscript section.** You will read in a lot of places
that Nature requires AI disclosure in the Methods. It does not. If you write to that
rule you are writing to a rule that does not exist, and you will assume you are covered
because you followed it.

## Your placement decision

Write this line at the top of your submission checklist, before you submit:

```
Venue: ____________________   Table row: ____________________
Statement goes in: ____________________
```

If you cannot say which row your venue falls under, you have not finished. That
decision is half the deliverable.

## Nature's tiers, for deciding what to disclose

**Green**, permitted and disclosure enhances trust: language polishing, structure and
formatting suggestions, comparing methodological options, stress-testing research
questions, and data cleaning and deduplication. Chapter 5's whole subject sits here
explicitly.

**Amber**, permitted with human oversight, verification and disclosure: suggesting
analytical or experimental approaches, drafting explanatory summaries, comparing
results to existing literature, extensive copy editing, pattern identification in
exploratory data analysis, recommending statistical tests. Most of what Chapters 6
through 8 do lives here.

**Red**, not permitted: generating hypotheses, analyses or conclusions and presenting
them as human-derived; fabricating data, citations or results; using an LLM to generate
core research reasoning without disclosure; assigning authorship or accountability to
AI systems or tools; delegating peer review to an LLM; creating photorealistic images.

Nature's stated core expectations tell you how the tiers get read at the margins:
"Human accountability is non-transferable." "AI may support, but must not replace,
scholarly judgement." "Transparency creates trust and confidence." And, for anyone
working with unpublished or sensitive material: "Confidentiality and data protection are
mandatory... Manuscripts, peer review reports, and sensitive data must not be shared
with unsecured or public AI systems."

## Grants

NIH's notice "Supporting Fairness and Originality in NIH Research Applications" states
that NIH will not consider applications substantially developed by AI, or containing
sections substantially developed by AI, to be original ideas of applicants. That is not
a disclosure requirement. It is a statement that AI-developed content does not count as
your idea. Enforcement named in the notice includes referral to the Office of Research
Integrity, disallowing costs, withholding future awards, suspension and possible
termination. The same notice caps applications at six per PI per calendar year across
all activity codes except T codes and R13.

Everything in this repo that touches a grant is on the assembly side, not the ideas
side: formatting, reference checking, consistency between the aims and the budget,
drafting a methods description of an analysis you designed and ran. The specific aims,
the hypothesis and the rationale have to be yours in the strong sense.

Nothing here gives you any compliance or regulatory coverage. No HIPAA claim, no
validated-system claim. See DISCLAIMER.md.

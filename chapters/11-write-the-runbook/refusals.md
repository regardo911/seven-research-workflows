Four moves when it happens, in the order to try them:

**Rephrase toward the mechanism and away from the organism.** A request framed around a
pathogen name carries risk signals that a request framed around the analysis does not.
"Compare expression between these two sample groups and produce the enrichment plot" is
the same task with the trigger removed, because the biology lives in your head and the
sample sheet, not in the prompt.

**Split the task.** Keep the biology on your side, send the computation across. In most
of the work in this book the model never needs the organism, the pathogen or the
disease. It needs a file path, a column name and a threshold.

**Move surfaces.** Refusal behaviour is not identical everywhere. If the desktop app
blocks a task, try the same task in Claude Code, where you are usually working on files
rather than asking questions about biology.

**Keep this record.** Two things come out of it. You discover which of your own
phrasings reliably trigger it. And if the pattern is bad enough to report or to raise
with your institution, you have evidence rather than an impression.

There is no refusal-rate figure in this file. The vendor has given two figures orders
of magnitude apart, so printing either would mislead. What is unambiguous is what they
said about it: they acknowledged making the wrong tradeoff, apologised for not
balancing the safeguards correctly, and conceded that users may see more false
positives while the classifiers are refined. You do not need a percentage on top of
that.

The one thing not to do is treat a refusal as a verdict on your work. A researcher at
UCSF hit refusals on herpesvirus and brain-tumour prompts while doing legitimate glioma
genetics. He was not doing anything biosecurity-related. Neither are you.

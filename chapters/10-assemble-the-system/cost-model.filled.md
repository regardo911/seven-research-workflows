Four things make a model like this survive a skeptical reader.

**It has a negative section.** The system costs time. A model with no costs in it is a
sales deck and anyone who has adopted a tool knows it, so leaving the costs out makes
the whole thing less credible rather than more.

**It totals per quarter, not per year.** A year is an abstraction and it inflates
everything by four. A quarter is a review cycle, which means the number is checkable
against something your PI actually experiences.

**Every rate is sourced.** The hourly figure names where it came from. If you are
estimating, say so, and use a conservative figure, because an inflated hourly rate is
the fastest way to have the whole model dismissed.

**It does not claim any discovery.** Not one line says the system found anything. Every
line is glue work displaced or verification added.

## Prices, and two numbers you will not find here

Pro is $20 a month billed monthly, or $17 a month billed annually. Max starts at $100 a
month, with tiers described as five times and twenty times the usage of Pro. Team is
$20 per seat per month billed annually, or $25 billed monthly, with a premium seat at
$100 annually or $125 monthly. Check the pricing page before you put any of these in a
document; they move.

There is no academic or nonprofit discount number in this file. The product page
carries no discount terms, no percentage and no eligibility rule, only a contact-sales
link, so there is nothing to print. Do not put a discount number in a model you are
going to show someone, because if they ask where it came from you will not have an
answer.

And no productivity multiplier. No 10x, no "three times faster". Those cannot be
sourced, they will be challenged, and the challenge is trivially easy to win.
